STARS
-----
stars.bin, star_names.json, star_info.bin
  HYG Database v4.1 (Hipparcos, Yale Bright Star, Gliese) — David Nash / astronexus
  https://github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database — CC BY-SA 4.0
  Modified: cut to magnitude 9.0, converted to binary. Shared under CC BY-SA 4.0.
  Eta Carinae corrected to magnitude 4.3 at its catalogue position.

  Tycho-2 Catalogue (telescope stars 9–13 mag, sky/deep_stars.bin) — Høg E. et al. (2000), A&A 355, L27
  Retrieved via VizieR (I/259, doi:10.26093/cds/vizier.1259).
  Stars matched to Gaia DR3 (gaiadr3.tycho2tdsc_merge_best_neighbour) use the Gaia
  magnitude, colour and position (propagated to J2000.0); no synthetic stars.
  Also contains the Gaia DR3 open-cluster and Magellanic Cloud stars listed below.

faint_stars.bin (open clusters, Large & Small Magellanic Clouds; magnitude 9–11)
  ESA Gaia DR3 — Gaia Collaboration, Vallenari et al. (2023), A&A 674, A1
  Data © ESA/Gaia/DPAC — CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO  https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia-users/license
  Retrieved via VizieR (I/355/gaiadr3, doi:10.26093/cds/vizier.1355) and the ESA Gaia Archive.
  G → V conversion: Riello et al. (2021), A&A 649, A3.
  The synthetic magnitude 9–11 background stars still stored in faint_stars.bin are a fallback only
  and are not drawn, as deep_stars.bin is always present.
  Shared under CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO.

  This work has made use of data from the European Space Agency (ESA) mission Gaia
  (https://www.cosmos.esa.int/gaia), processed by the Gaia Data Processing and Analysis
  Consortium (DPAC, https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dpac/consortium). Funding for the
  DPAC has been provided by national institutions, in particular the institutions
  participating in the Gaia Multilateral Agreement.
  This research has made use of the VizieR catalogue access tool, CDS, Strasbourg, France
  (doi:10.26093/cds/vizier).

Variable stars (Algol, Mira)
  General Catalogue of Variable Stars — Samus et al. (2017), Astronomy Reports 61, 80 (VizieR B/gcvs)

Close double stars (stars.bin)
  The Washington Visual Double Star Catalog (WDS) — Mason B.D. et al. (2001), AJ 122, 3466
  Retrieved via VizieR (B/wds). Used to split HYG stars into components: separation, position angle
  and component magnitudes from the latest WDS measurement.
  Companions already present in HYG (e.g. Sirius B) are moved to the latest WDS
  separation and position angle.

Gaia star pack (separate download: cheonsang-extra-1.0.1.jar, which also carries the
  Milky Way 16K/32K tiles and the 8K Moon map)
  ESA Gaia DR3 gaia_source, all-sky, G < 15.5 (32,061,510 stars with 9 < V <= 15.2)
  Data © ESA/Gaia/DPAC — CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO. Retrieved from the ESA Gaia Archive.
  Modified: G converted to Johnson V (Riello et al. 2021), BP−RP to B−V (fitted on Tycho-2 matches),
  positions propagated to J2000.0, binned by sky cell. Also contains the Tycho-2 stars of the base mod.
  Shared under CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO. The pack carries its own CREDITS.txt.

famous_stars.json (18 named stars without a traditional proper name, e.g. Proxima Centauri,
Barnard's Star, Tabby's Star, Cygnus X-1)
  Positions, magnitudes and distances from HYG Database v4.1 (above) — CC BY-SA 4.0.
  Tabby's Star (KIC 8462852) and UY Scuti are not in HYG; their positions are the SIMBAD (CDS, Strasbourg) ICRS values.
  This research has made use of the SIMBAD database, operated at CDS, Strasbourg, France.
  Short descriptions (Korean and English): itsnotfineart. Shared under CC BY-SA 4.0.

DEEP-SKY OBJECTS
----------------
dso.json (Messier, Caldwell, 69 objects inside the Magellanic Clouds, and 264 more galaxies to V 11)
  OpenNGC — Mattia Verga — CC BY-SA 4.0 — https://github.com/mattiaverga/OpenNGC
  Galaxy Hubble types (dso_morph.json) and radial-velocity distances (dso_dist.json, v / 70 km/s/Mpc) from OpenNGC.
  Eyepiece sketches of galaxies: galaxy_atlas.png is made from Digitized Sky Survey II (DSS2 red) images
  (see below); galaxies without an image are drawn procedurally from those types.
  Nebulae, planetary nebulae and globular clusters too; nebulae combine DSS2 red, DSS2 blue and
  (where the DSS2 plate has a seam) DSS1 red images.
  Globular cluster core radii (shape of the central concentration) for 13 clusters from
  Harris W.E. (1996, 2010 edition), AJ 112, 1487 — Catalog of Parameters for Milky Way Globular Clusters.
  The Ring Nebula (M57) is drawn as a ring shape, as its DSS plate is saturated.

Galaxy sketch atlas (textures/sky/galaxy_atlas.png)
  The Digitized Sky Surveys were produced at the Space Telescope Science Institute under U.S. Government
  grant NAG W-2166. The images of these surveys are based on photographic data obtained using the Oschin
  Schmidt Telescope on Palomar Mountain and the UK Schmidt Telescope. The plates were processed into the
  present compressed digital form with the permission of these institutions.
  The Second Palomar Observatory Sky Survey (POSS-II) was made by the California Institute of Technology with
  funds from the National Science Foundation, the National Geographic Society, the Sloan Foundation, the
  Samuel Oschin Foundation, and the Eastman Kodak Corporation.
  POSS-II plates: copyright (c) 1993-2003 California Institute of Technology.
  The National Geographic Society - Palomar Observatory Sky Atlas (POSS-I) was made by the California Institute
  of Technology with grants from the National Geographic Society (DSS1 images).
  UK Schmidt plates: copyright (c) 1993-2004 Anglo-Australian Observatory Board.
  Data via MAST (STScI), retrieved with NASA SkyView (GSFC). License: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
  (https://archive.stsci.edu/dss/copyright.html). Modified: background removed, stars removed, rotated,
  resampled, converted to log brightness, plates combined, saturated cores reshaped. galaxy_atlas.png is shared under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial).

dso_dist.json, dso_morph.json
  Caldwell numbers: Wikipedia "Caldwell catalogue" — CC BY-SA 4.0
  Distances and galaxy types from Wikipedia ("Messier object", "Caldwell catalogue",
  individual galaxy articles) — CC BY-SA 4.0. Shared under CC BY-SA 4.0.

MOON, PLANETS, SMALL BODIES
---------------------------
moon_map*.png
  NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio — CGI Moon Kit (LRO data) — https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720
  NASA imagery, not copyrighted in the US (NASA media guidelines). Modified: resized, toned.
  lroc_color_poles_4k: 4096, 2048, 1024 and 256 px versions.
  cheonsang-extra: lroc_color_poles_8k, moon_map_8k.png, 8192 px.
  Elevation (2.0.2, crater relief): NASA LRO LOLA elevation model, CGI Moon Kit ldem_16 (16 px/degree)
  https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720 — NASA data, not copyrighted in the US.
  Modified: resampled to each map size; east/north slope and height stored in the G, B, A channels
  of moon_map_1k.png, moon_map.png, moon_map_4k.png and (cheonsang-extra 1.0.2) moon_map_8k.png.

moon_names.json
  Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature — IAU WGPSN / USGS Astrogeology Science Center
  https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov — US Government work, public domain.
  Korean names: itsnotfineart.

mars_map.png
  Solar System Scope (INOVE) "8k_mars" — CC BY 4.0 — https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/
  (based on NASA data). Modified: resized to 2048×1024.

jupiter.png, saturn.png, saturn_ring*.png
  Solar System Scope (INOVE) "8k_jupiter", "8k_saturn", "8k_saturn_ring_alpha" — CC BY 4.0
  https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/ (based on NASA data).
  Modified: averaged along longitude (band colours only), contrast and saturation reduced, rendered as discs;
  ring profile rescaled to Saturn radii and rendered face-on.
  2.0.2: smaller copies (jupiter_64…512, saturn_64…512, saturn_ring*_128…1024) with softened disc edge.

Saturn's moons, asteroids (asteroids.bin), rotation checks
  NASA/JPL Horizons System — https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/ — US Government data.
  Saturn's moons (Titan, Rhea, Iapetus, Tethys, Dione, Enceladus, Mimas): orbit formulas
  fitted to Horizons ephemerides for 1900–2050 (only the fitted coefficients are bundled).

Great Red Spot longitude trend
  JUPOS (amateur observation database, H.-J. Mettig et al.) — factual values only.


MILKY WAY
---------
milkyway*.png, mw_*.png (Magellanic Clouds, M24, Coalsack patches)
  NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio — Deep Star Maps 2020
  https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851 — NASA imagery, not copyrighted in the US.
  Modified: tone-mapped, resized, cropped. The telescope view uses milkyway_2020_32k reduced to 12K.
  With the Gaia star pack, the starlight of the newly drawn stars is subtracted from the map per sky
  cell (Gaia DR3 HEALPix star counts), so the same light is not added twice.
  cheonsang-extra and Ultra Pack tiles (eye16, eye32, lx16): milkyway_2020_32k, cut into 1024 px tiles, catalogue stars removed.

H-alpha layer in tiles/lx16 and the Ultra Pack milkyway*.png (long exposure)
  Finkbeiner, D. P. 2003, "A Full-Sky H-alpha Template for Microwave Foreground Prediction", ApJS 146, 407
  Data from NASA LAMBDA — https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/product/foreground/fg_halpha_get.html
  Modified: reprojected to equatorial coordinates, tone-mapped, coloured red and added to the long-exposure Milky Way.

METHODS (formulas used, no data copied)
---------------------------------------
  J. Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, 2nd ed. (positions, phases, libration, Jupiter's moons)
  VSOP87 (Bretagnon & Francou 1988, A&A 202, 309; IMCCE) — planet positions, truncated series
  (coefficients read from the PyMeeus package listing of VSOP87D)
  Krisciunas & Schaefer (1991), PASP 103, 1033 — moonlit sky brightness
  Schaefer (1990), PASP 102, 212 — sky brightness units
  Kasten & Young (1989), Applied Optics 28, 4735 — airmass
  Sæmundsson (1986), Bennett (1982) — atmospheric refraction
  IAU WGCCRE (Archinal et al. 2011/2018) — rotation of Mars and Jupiter
